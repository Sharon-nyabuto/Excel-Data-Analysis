# Food Basket Sales Performance Analysis Report

>Date: 10/05/206 | Data Analysis Team | Period Covered in Analysis: Jan – Nov 2023

## Overview

**Food Basket** is a retail store chain in Kenya that specializes in the sale of everyday consumer goods. It has successfully built a customer base across multiple regions, setting a solid foundation for expansion. The business is seeking to ensure sustainable growth and maximum optimization of resource allocation in their expansion efforts. This analysis will provide data to guide decisions such as product refinement and specialization, key areas of investment and marketing strategies.

## Data Cleaning and Preparation
For accurate analysis and data quality, the following data cleaning steps were performed;

* Removed duplicate records and verified dataset for missing values.
* Corrected inconsistencies and spelling errors within the dataset. 
* Standardized text fields for uniform formatting using functions such as `TRIM()` and `PROPER()`. 
* Formatted columns into appropriate data types for accurate analysis.

## Executive Summary
The business generated a total revenue of **KES. 270,767.80** from 320 orders, reflecting a moderately high transaction volume with a mid-range average order value of **KES. 846.15**.

Meru recorded the highest sales at approximately KES. 42K, while Nakuru and Kisii recorded the lowest sales at below Kes. 22K. 

**Trend analysis** showed sales fluctuated throughout the year, peaking at around **29K in May**, before declining to nearly 15K in November. 

**Correlation analysis** revealed an almost non-existent relationship between discounts and sales, suggesting discounts had limited impact on revenue growth. Additionally, the average product quality rating of 3.2 out of 5 highlighted concerns about customer satisfaction with product quality.

## Key Insights

### Regional Sales Analysis
Sales performance across regions showed a notable variation in performance. **Meru** recorded the highest sales at approximately KES. 42,000 followed by **Eldoret** at around KES. 34,000 while**Nakuru and Kisii** generated the lowest sales figures, both below KES. 22,000. Customer distribution appears to have influenced these results, as Meru and Eldoret also had the highest customer count. 

**Nairobi** had a relatively high customer base but generated lower sales than expected, suggesting lower average customer spending compared to top-performing regions. Kisii had the least customers alongside low sales performance, highlighting the impact of limited customer volume on revenue generation. 

Overall, the findings suggest that regional sales performance is influenced by both the number of customers and differences in transaction value across regions.

### Customer Age Analysis
The **38-47 age band** (which also represents the highest number of customers) accounted for 23% of the total revenue. Strong revenue performance is also maintained across the **48–57 ages,** indicating *consistent purchasing power* among middle-aged and older adult customers. The 18–27 segment generates revenue levels nearly equal to the 58–67 group, suggesting strong spending potential among younger customers. 

There is a **notable decline** in both customer count and revenue within the 28–37 bracket compared to adjacent age groups. This could point to a retention or engagement gap within the group. The 68–77 group has the least revenue and smallest customer base. This may be influenced by lifestyle and mobility factors associated with older age groups.

### Trend and Correlation Analysis
The analysis revealed a weak relationship between discounts and sales as higher discounts did not consistently lead to higher sales. Discounts alone are therefore not effective in predicting or improving sales performance at Food Basket.

Further sales analysis showed a seasonal pattern. There was high performance in **January, May, and August** compared to adjacent months. Sales showed a decline in the last quarter, visible from September onwards, meaning customer spending slows towards year end.

## Recommendations
1.	Analyze success drivers in the best performing regions i.e Machakos and Eldoret, and replicate them to the lower performing ones, like Nakuru and Kisii where feasible. 
2.	Perform local market analysis to determine regional trends in terms of customer spending and satisfaction, then tailor marketing strategies based on this information.
3.	Investigate factors contributing to the moderate average product quality rating to identify opportunities for improving customer satisfaction and product performance.
4.	Move away from across-the-board discounts and adopt more strategic and targeted promotions.
5.	Use seasonal sales patterns to guide forecasting, marketing and promotional activities throughout the year.
